Anne Brontë

Feminist Pioneer

Next: Personal Letter Excerpts →

— 1820 — Born at Thornton, Yorkshire
— 1825 — Death of her two eldest sisters which brought her closer to Emily and Charlotte.
— 1835 — Attended Roe Head School
— 1839 — Began her six years working as a governess with a disasterous first position which inspired Agnes Grey
— 1847 — Published Agnes Grey
— 1847 — Published The Tenant of Wildfell Hall
— 1848 — Died aged 29 in Scarborough of Tuberculosis having been brought there by Charlotte in the hopes of recovery

Anne Brontë was a precocious child who, when asked by her father Patrick what she most desired, replied, “Age and experience.” For most of her life, she shared a bedroom with her aunt, Elizabeth Branwell, which is thought to have contributed to her strong Anglican moral convictions.

The world of Gondal was a fictional creation of Anne and Emily. It drew from their reading of Homer, Shakespeare, Lord Byron, and others from their father’s library. This collaboration created an incredibly strong bond between the two sisters, which endured throughout their short lives.

When Emily withdrew from Roe Head School for Clergy Children due to extreme homesickness, Anne took her place. She was keen to acquire the education needed for a position as a governess, which would allow her to support herself independently.

Upon leaving Roe Head, she took a job as a governess at Blake Hall in Mirfield. The children there were spoiled and disobedient, and she struggled to educate them. Their parents did not want her to discipline them, and after nine months, unhappy with their progress, they dismissed Anne. This period of her life formed the basis for her novel Agnes Grey.

Anne had more success in her second position at Thorp Green Hall near York. All three Brontë sisters worked as governesses or teachers, but Anne’s six years in the role made her the most successful of the three. She is also thought to have held a quiet affection, maybe even love, for her father’s curate, William Weightman.

However, like her sister Emily, Anne contracted tuberculosis. Charlotte brought her to Scarborough, a favourite place of Anne’s, in the hope that the sea air would help her recover. Her condition worsened, and shortly before her death she whispered to Charlotte to “take courage.” She passed away soon after and was buried in the seaside town she loved.

“I wished to tell the truth, for truth always conveys its own moral to those who are able to receive it.”

— Anne Brontë, The Tenant of Wildfell Hall
