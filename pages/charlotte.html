Charlotte Brontë

Celebrity authoress of her time

Next: Meet Emily Brontë →

— 1816 — Born at Thornton, Yorkshire
— 1821 — Attended Cowan Bridge School for clergy children
— 1842 — Joined the Pensionnat Heger, Brussels as a tutor
— 1847 — Published Jane Eyre
— 1853 — Published Villette
— 1854 — Married Arthur Bell Nicholls
— 1855 — Died aged 38 along with her unborn child due to severe morning sickness

Charlotte Brontë was the only one of the three sisters to experience lasting fame and recognition in her lifetime. She became a close friend of fellow author Elizabeth Gaskell and mixed with London literary society, including the widely lauded William Thackeray, who was a disappointment to her when they met in person.

She had an incredibly strong moral compass, influenced heavily by her Anglican faith, although even there she was she was characteristically introspective and critical, once remarking sardonically that she knew clergymen too well to consider any of them infallible.

Her early writing with her brother Branwell and her sisters about the imaginary kingdom of Angria showed a remarkable imagination. This endured throughout her short but extraordinary life.

She was fiercely independent, and in later life, after her sisters had passed, she took sole charge of preserving their literary legacy.

An intensely shy but emotionally passionate woman, she struggled socially, even though she longed for love and connection, which she eventually and briefly found with her father’s curate, Arthur Bell Nicholls. But unfortunately the Brontë family lineage died with her.

“I am no bird; and no net ensnares me: I am a free human being with an independent will.”

— Charlotte Brontë, Jane Eyre
