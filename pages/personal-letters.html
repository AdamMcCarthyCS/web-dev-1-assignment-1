Personal Correspondence of the Brontë Sisters

A view into their inner world

Next: TBA →

Letter writing was the most common form of long distance communication in the Victorian Era. Travelling longer distances, even 20-30 miles, was an arduous task accomplished in an uncomfortable horsedrawn stagecoach. Hence friends and business associates alike spent a significant amount of time writing longhand correspondence. This turned out to be a fantastic thing for posterity as we have the benefit of reading the thoughts of the people that shaped that era in their formal and informal letters. Much of todays scholarship on Victorian authors is derived from the day-to-day communication they made with others. The Brontë sisters are no different and a look at some excerpts from their letters gives us a glimpse into their inner worlds.

Charlotte Brontë's determination to preserve her sisters legacies

“...In Emily’s nature the extremes of vigour and simplicity seemed to meet. Under an unsophisticated culture, inartificial tastes, and an unpretending outside, lay a secret power and fire that might have informed the brain and kindled the veins of a hero...”

“..Anne’s character was milder and more subdued; she wanted the power, the fire, the originality of her sister, but was well endowed with quiet virtues of her own. Long-suffering, self-denying, reflective, and intelligent, a constitutional reserve and taciturnity placed and kept her in the shade, and covered her mind, and especially her feelings, with a sort of nun-like veil, which was rarely lifted....”

— Charlotte Brontë, Biographical notice of Ellis and Acton Bell
