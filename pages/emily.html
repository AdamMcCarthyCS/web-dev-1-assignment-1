Emily Brontë

No Coward Soul is Mine

Next: Meet Anne Brontë →

Emily Brontë started writing early, creating the world of Gondal with her sister, Anne. This imaginary world kept developing between them well into their adulthood, helping Emily hone the skill of writing scenes of violence, betrayal, and intrigue that featured, in a different setting and time, in Wuthering Heights.

She was known to be physically strong and would walk for miles among the heather of the moors. This uncontainable backdrop mirrors the romance between Catherine and Heathcliff in Wuthering Heights, the overpowering nature in her poetry, and her own fierce, independent spirit.

Emily had almost no friends outside of her immediate family. She refused to conform to the expectations of Victorian women or to be a form of entertainment. This theme can be seen in the characters of Wuthering Heights, who follow their own moral codes rather than those imposed on them by society.

Death was a constant shadow over the Brontë household. The Parsonage was surrounded by a graveyard so overcrowded that the village water supply was poisoned. Emily's mother, sisters, and aunt had died while she was young. The supernatural featured prominently in Wuthering Heights as a force that couldn't separate those who loved each other.

Emily remains one of the great enigmas of English literature. While Charlotte described her as "stronger than a man, simpler than a child," she was deeply introspective. Some argue that in her novel, Hindley's descent into addiction and Heathcliff's into madness were both inspired by her brother Branwell's dissolution through addiction, an event she lived through firsthand, while also taking on the responsibility of being his primary carer.

— 1818 — Emily Jane Brontë is born in Thornton, West Yorkshire
— 1825 — Maria and Elizabeth Brontë die, drawing Emily, Anne, and Charlotte closer together
— 1836 — Emily attends Roe Head School for clergy daughters briefly
— 1842 — Travels to Brussels with Charlotte to study languages but returns home, homesick for Haworth
— 1846 — Her first published work in Poems by Currer, Ellis, and Acton Bell, receives strong praise
— 1847 — Publishes Wuthering Heights using pseudonym Ellis Bell
— 1848 — Dies at age 30 from tuberculosis

"I've dreamt in my life dreams that have stayed with me ever after, and changed my ideas: they've gone through and through me, like wine through water, and altered the colour of my mind."

— Emily Brontë, Wuthering Heights
